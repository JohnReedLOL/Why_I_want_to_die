# Why_I_want_to_die
An essay explaining why I want to die. Submission of this essay is mandatory in order for me to eventually receive physician assisted suicide in Switzerland, which is how I want to ultimately die.

To whom it may concern, 

This essay is to explain why I, John Michael Reed, wish to die. My death wish is not a matter of depression. Regardless of whether or not I am depressed, my wish to die steadfastly persists. It is also not a matter of insanity. I take my Extended Release Lithium (mood stabilizer) and my Abilify (antipsychotic) medications every day without missing a day and I am sane. My bipolar schizophrenia (or bipolar disorder with psychotic features) is treated. I am not manic, depressed, or psychotic. So what justification do I have for wanting to die? 

Before I can answer that question in a way that a variety of different people would appreciate, it is important to understand that different people sympathize/empathize with different things. Something that instills grief or pity in one person might not instill grief or pity in another person who is looking at the same thing or the same human misfortune. Before continuing with this essay, please take a look at this YouTube video by a medical doctor talking about grief in patients filing for disability benefits caused by personal limitations: 

- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=talaainLcUg 

In the YouTube video, the doctor talks about how strong, macho, physically fit, blue-collar men who become physically disabled grieve their loss of physical function much more than people who do white-collar, intellectual work. On the flip side, people who do work that mainly involves their brain more heavily grieve the loss of brain functions like concentration and memory. 

Me personally, I started walking with a walking stick or cane around year 2019. In addition to that more physical disability, I also developed cognitive issues. The cognitive issues like loss of concentration and (a form of) memory bothered me much more than the physical ones like needing a cane. I like to do things with my brain, and I wasn't able to do things with my brain anymore, and that made me sad. For example, I have some books that I would like to read, and I used to enjoy reading hundreds of pages in a day, but nowadays I can’t read more than a few pages before giving up, and that makes me feel self-pity. That being said, whenever I saw someone with a cane, I didn't feel much pity for them because to me, a cane is no big deal. That being said, a strong, macho, physically fit, blue-collar man who sees someone with a cane or who ends up needing a cane might be much more psychologically affected by that. Different people sympathize/empathize with different things. 

That being said, in the future, I ultimately want to be approved for physician assisted suicide in Switzerland (where that’s legal). The approval won’t happen right away, but I’d like it to happen eventually (in the meantime I just want to get a Do Not Resuscitate and/or an Advance Directive in). Now, some right-to-die organizations in Switzerland require applicants to write an essay explaining why they wish to die, and this essay is then read and used to make a decision about whether to approve or deny the wish to die. I am writing and sharing this essay now, but the problem with this essay is, again, different people sympathize/empathize with different things. If the reader is more physically oriented, they may more appreciate physical issues of mine like needing a cane. I used to be so flexible that with straight legs, I could bend forwards and extend my hands several inches past my toes, but now with my rigidity my hands only go down to my knees, so putting on socks is a struggle. I used to participate in group yoga for fun, but now if I want to do that I have to take anti-Parkinson's medication like Amantadine beforehand, and it's not worth it (Amantadine is dopaminergic, which can destabilize my bipolar, so nowadays I just don't do yoga anymore). I used to enjoy running half-marathons (13 miles, 21 kilometers) but now I struggle to walk one mile (maybe two miles with walking sticks). I used to ride my bicycle for miles, but I have given up on that because I am no longer able to comfortably ride a bicycle with my rigidity and pseudo-Parkinsonism. Anyway, if the reader loves to drive, they may more sympathize/empathize with the fact that I can’t drive (there was a time when I could, but not anymore). If the reader is a computer programmer like I was, they may deeply sympathize/empathize with not being able to do that work anymore due to brain-related issues (also due to muscle and postural rigidity I can no longer sit upright comfortably at a computer desk, virtually all of my electronic device use nowadays is on my smartphone). Different people sympathize/empathize with different things, and depending on what they sympathize/empathize with, a different focus for my explanation is more appropriate. 

All the things I mentioned are bothersome, but to me, for me, they are not my main, personal reason for wanting to die. Maybe if another person had those issues it would be their reason, but it’s not my reason, or at least not my main reason. Before I explain my main reason, I’d like you to watch this short YouTube video by psychiatrist Dr. K: 

https://youtube.com/shorts/PCtpYTiyoP0 

In the video, Dr. K says “50% of men who kill themselves have no history or evidence of mental illness”. He also mentions men “objectively realizing that there’s no way out of this situation, so they turn to suicide” and that the #1 thing associated with male suicide is “a sense of thwarted belongingness”. 

What does “thwarted belongingness” mean? In the video, he talks about men trying to connect and getting rejected. I’d like to tell a little story about a male friend/acquaintance of mine who got divorced not long ago. The divorce caused him to have suicidal thoughts. That to me is kind of like what “thwarted belongingness” means. Man cannot connect, forced disconnection (especially involving a woman, like your wife leaving you). 

Now, before we go on, it’s important to understand something about me. When I was getting approved for disability benefits, the Social Security Administration (SSA) employee wrote down in the records that I have “marked” issues interacting with others, that “marked limitations in social functioning are present”, that I have “social interaction limitations”. Laypeople who know me have described me as “like a cross between narcissism and autism”. My therapist, Dr. Byk, told me that I have "no boundaries" and she leaned more towards me having autism than narcissism, but I don’t believe I meet the diagnostic criteria for autism. In high school my nickname was “Sheldon” from the TV sitcom The Big Bang Theory because of my personality. Not long ago I took a test of affective/emotional empathy called the Toronto Empathy Questionnaire ( https://psychology-tools.com/test/toronto-empathy-questionnaire ) and I scored virtually zero on it (out of like 64). One woman who I generally like and who I had sex with in the past left me and wrote “You don’t know what others are going through, and based on your social media posts, you don’t seem to care”. My social media posts generally don’t get any interaction and have been described as “thinking out loud without mental awareness of others”. But yeah, that same woman who I generally like and who I had sex with in the past mentioned me having some sort of problem with sympathy and/or some form of empathy and/or care, I don’t remember exactly which it was. But yeah, there’s some sort of personality and/or “neurodivergence” problem that I have. On at least one occasion I sent a message to a woman saying “I love you” and she replied “You love me? What? No… it’s all in your head, dude” and that really hurt me. To this day, with my issue with sympathy, some form of empathy, and/or care, I don’t know if what I consider “love” is real, or at least can be perceived as real love by the other person. Maybe I lack a form of love that I call “concerned love”, I don't know, it's just a theory or a hypothesis. Anyway, going back to what I was saying four sentences prior about there being some sort of personality and/or “neurodivergence” problem, my therapist, Dr. Byk, leans more towards calling what I have a “neurodivergence” but I lean more towards calling it a personality issue, but whatever. Anyway, it’s a problem and I’ve tried everything for it. 

But first, why is it a problem? I first fell in love/infatuation at age 13. She was a friend named “Cathy”. We used to hang out in a private part of the school, just the two of us. I don’t know exactly when I developed the “neurodivergence” or personality issue, but basically she ended up dating my friend Andrew instead of me. I was madly in love/infatuation with Cathy. Despite not wanting to, I regularly fall in strong love/infatuation like I did with my grade school friend Cathy. This love/infatuation never works out and one way or the other always ends in me experiencing psychological pain. I fall in love/infatuation very hard and the psychological pain is significant. It hurts a lot. It hurts me more than needing a cane or not being able to program a computer or not being able to drive. It hurts not being able to have romantic love. It is my "sense of thwarted belongingness", as Dr. K put it in his YouTube video. I’ve tried about six psychologists, eight psychiatrists, and 5 neurologists. I’ve tried about 30 different neurological and/or psychiatric medications in my life. Nobody can fix me and nothing can fix me and I’ve had enough. 

I want to reiterate this. Something that instills grief or pity in one person might not install grief or pity in another who is looking at the same thing or the same human misfortune. One man might say “Oh, I’ve had plenty of sex in my long life and I don’t think sex is a big deal, men who cry about no sex are big babies”. He might not appreciate one of MY reasons for wanting to die, but maybe he appreciates needing a cane or being unable to drive or being unable to do mental tasks or something like that. You can’t draw equivalence between different people’s internal, mental suffering. That’s actually a quote/rule from NAMI (National Alliance on Mental Illness) support groups (talk groups). Like you can’t unbiasedly say Bob has schizophrenia which causes internal, mental suffering that is worse than that caused by Alice’s bipolar which causes internal, mental suffering that is worse than that caused by Fred’s OCD. There are too many factors in play, and comparing one person's internal, mental suffering to another is like comparing apples to oranges. Or to put it another way, “suffering is a subjective experience that can only be quantified by the patient and cannot be objectively compared across individuals” (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9306695/ ).

Anyway, I have a lot of different disabilities. If I were a singer or musician I would grieve about being musically tone deaf (note that I actually am pretty much musically tone deaf, and that disappoints me when I try to do karaoke, but I can live with that). If I were a yoga teacher I would grieve about being unable to do yoga (note that due to muscle rigidity and pseudo-Parkinsonism I can’t do yoga, but it’s not a huge deal for me in and of itself). If I were a macho, strong, blue-collar laboring man I would grieve about needing a cane. If I were a stunt driver I would grieve about not being able to drive a car, even after having taken a driving course. If I were an anti-government libertarian I would grieve about living off government disability benefits instead of being self-sufficient. But none of those things (all of them relevant) are my main, personal reason for wanting to die; they’re not the biggest thing that I grieve about. My main, personal reason for wanting to die is the repeated psychological pain of crushed infatuation and the thing that I grieve the most about is my absence of any romantic relationship despite wanting one. I know that sounds like a trivial pain, but for me it’s not trivial and I don’t appreciate the comparisons with other limitations. It’s like comparing the internal, mental suffering associated with Bob’s schizophrenia with the internal, mental suffering associated with Alice’s bipolar with the internal, mental suffering associated with Fred’s OCD. You are not in a position to say who has it worse or which is worse. 

In addition, different things affect different people differently. For example, the now deceased physicist Stephen Hawking cheated on his wife while he was a quadriplegic and then married his mistress. He wrote a bestselling book by using tiny movements to control a digital keyboard. He had more sex as a quadriplegic than I had in my entire life, and he made more money as a quadriplegic than I made in my entire life. He had ALS (Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis, also known as Lou Gehrig's disease) and had more success with women and more career success with ALS than I ever had in my life. I would seriously consider trading lives with him if that were even possible. But yeah, maybe one person gets ALS and starts sleeping around and making millions of dollars in his wheelchair and another person is totally destroyed. I have the disabilities I have, and they basically totally destroy my entire life, even if they might not seem as severe as Stephen Hawking’s ALS. Comparing my disabilities to those of Stephen Hawking is like comparing Bob’s schizophrenia with Alice’s bipolar. You are not in a position to say who has it worse or which is worse, even if on the surface a motorized wheelchair (which Stephen Hawking used) looks worse than a cane (which I used). Stephen Hawking progressively lost muscle function, but other than that, his brain was intact, and I can't say the same for me. 

Anyway, my personal beliefs also play a role in my desire to die. I am an adamant supporter of the right to die when one is ready. I believe people have a biographical story to their life. They go to school, date, get married, work, have kids (assuming they want that), build their career, become grandparents, maybe move into assisted living in old age or something like that. Eventually an individual’s biographical story comes to an end. If they spend all day rotting in front of the television, that is not biographical-story plot. They are in the epilogue of their life; their life (their life story) is effectively over. It’s not like they’re going to win the Nobel prize or something while watching a rerun of a soap opera on television for the 100th time. They’re done. Productivity isn’t just finished, the mere hope of productivity, any sort of future productivity, ever, is completely extinguished. Enough watching television, it’s time for the individual in question to die, or at least that’s what I believe. 

In parts of Europe like Switzerland, they have a concept called "Balance Sheet Suicide" (derived from the German term Bilanzsuizid), which refers to a rational suicide where an individual concludes that their life is no longer worth living after a sober, "accounting-like" assessment of their circumstances. If all you are doing is rotting in front of the television all day with nobody visiting you, I think a rational assessment can conclude that life is no longer worth living. 

Now me, I am doing very little. I don’t own a television, but I am mostly rotting in front of YouTube on my phone all day. I can’t even watch most YouTube videos because my focus is not long enough. I end up limiting myself to “Shorts”, or videos that are a couple minutes in length. It’s not that I don’t want to watch longer videos, it’s just that I am not able to. Again, my focus is not long enough. But yeah, in terms of productivity, I am (for the most part) done doing absolutely anything. I do not believe that I will ever return to work. I do not believe that I will ever have a wife, fiancée, girlfriend, or other such long-term partner. I do not believe I will ever have biological children (despite wanting them for pretty much my entire life). My disabilities (many of them congenital) are not curable. My life is done. All I have that’s productive is a couple hours a week of volunteer teaching a computer class for senior citizens, and that’ll get boring eventually (I taught another sort of similar class in the past and got bored of it eventually). But yeah, as far as the “Balance Sheet” of my life goes, there are very little “pros”/”assets” relative to “cons”/”liabilities”. I believe, given the hopeless circumstances, that I can make a rational decision to die. Nobody in Switzerland said they will approve me until I’m 70 at the earliest, but if I could at least just get a Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) and/or an Advance Directive that would be nice. Note that my primary care doctor refuses to sign my DNR, but whatever.
